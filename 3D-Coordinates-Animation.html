3D Coordinate Systems

Visualising and Plotting Points in Three Dimensions

Standard 3D Orientation (IB Convention)
  • x-axis (Red): Comes out towards you (depth).
  • y-axis (Green): Horizontal line pointing right (width).
  • z-axis (Blue): Vertical line pointing up (height).
  • Origin (0,0,0): The central intersection point where all three axes meet.
Plotting a Point: P(3, 4, 5)

To locate point P(3, 4, 5) from origin (0,0,0):

  1. Move 3 units out along the x-axis.
  2. Move 4 units right parallel to the y-axis.
  3. Move 5 units up parallel to the z-axis.
Key Formula: 3D Midpoint
Midpoint Formula

Given two points A(x1, y1, z1) and B(x2, y2, z2), the midpoint M is:

M = (
x1 + x22
,
y1 + y22
,
z1 + z22
)
Worked Example

Find the midpoint between A(1, 2, 3) and B(5, 6, 7):

M = (
1 + 52
,
2 + 62
,
3 + 72
) = (3, 4, 5)
IB Exam Practice: Ski Resort Cable Car
Real-Life Application

A straight ski lift cable connects a base valley station A(2, 1, 3) to a summit station B(10, 13, 15). Coordinates are measured in hundreds of metres.

Question:
A mountain café M is to be built exactly halfway along the cable car route. Find the 3D coordinates of café M.

Solution: Ski Resort Midpoint
Worked Solution

Apply the 3D Midpoint formula to A(2, 1, 3) and B(10, 13, 15):

M = (
2 + 102
,
1 + 132
,
3 + 152
)
M = (
122
,
142
,
182
) = (6, 7, 9)
Interpretation

The mountain café M is located at coordinate position (6, 7, 9).

Summary & Key Takeaways
  • 3D coordinates extend 2D geometry by adding the z-axis (vertical height).
  • Always follow the vector component order: (x, y, z).
  • Movement starts at the origin (0,0,0) and travels sequentially along each axis direction.
  • The midpoint formula is simply the average of each set of coordinates.
